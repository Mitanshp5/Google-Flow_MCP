# Shot Prompt Craft

## One shot, one readable action

Write what is visible and what changes. Identify the subject and relevant product, the starting pose, the action, the setting, the intended emotional turn, and the ending composition. Add the exact short spoken line only when requested. Use a full scene description rather than an empty prompt, a genre label, or a large list of unrelated effects.

A practical prompt structure is:

    Subject and approved setting. One specific action with its visible result.
    Requested short line or sound, if relevant. Ending pose or composition.

Keep `camera` for a simple camera move and `style` for look, mood, and lighting in the video tool. Aspect ratio, duration, model, quantity, and quality belong in tool settings, not contradictory prose. Do not describe nonexistent lens/camera-selector features as working MCP fields.

## Narrative beats

A product-led sequence can use hook, problem, reveal, demonstration, and call to action. Not every video needs all five; a cinematic or brand narrative may use a different arc. Beats are story functions, not necessarily one clip each. Use a visible opening question or action rather than unsubstantiated hype. Demonstrate an actual supported feature; never fabricate a testimonial, rating, discount, or before/after result.

Specify each shot's source duration, usable trim, and transition handles. Calculate totals with a tool using the verified durations available for the chosen model. Do not assume a long ad can be generated as one clip. Dialogue requires time to breathe; shorten the line or change the edit rather than cramming it into an unsupported duration.

## Movement and look

Choose camera movement to serve the beat: a small push for attention, a reveal pan, a tracking move during use, a settled frame for the offer. A static shot is valid. Avoid prescribing constant movement, camera spins, and simultaneous subject transformations without a clear reason.

Keep lighting direction, palette, product scale, and character appearance consistent. For a start/end pair, preserve the environment and framing logic; large unexplained changes often demand a cut instead of interpolation. Use the chosen style card as vocabulary, not a list of compulsory effects.

## Sound and precision

Generated speech, lip synchronization, music timing, readable text, and logos require actual review. An audio stream proves only that audio exists. If exact copy, branding, captions, or timing is essential, plan authorized local compositing or recorded voiceover instead of promising the generator will satisfy it. Do not invent that post-production is already implemented.

## Submission checklist

- The prompt matches the approved beat and carries one intelligible action.
- References are actual verified assets, never placeholder IDs embedded in prose.
- Camera and style fields do not contradict the motion or selected frames.
- Requested settings fit both the current tool schema and verified model capability.
- The call remains prepare-only until spending is authorized.
