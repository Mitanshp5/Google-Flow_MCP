# Food and Beverage

## Hook
Choose one appetizing action: a pour, a tear, a slice, a steam reveal, or a finished serving. Make the actual dish or drink identifiable before adding effects.

## Camera
Use a stable close-up for texture, a short reveal for presentation, or a top-down view when preparation steps matter. Keep hand positions, vessel size, and ingredient placement coherent.

## Lighting
Use soft directional or back light to show texture and believable freshness. Keep color faithful to the approved product. Reflections and steam should support the food, not conceal it.

## Beat
Show the ingredient or preparation action, its visible result, and a settled serving view. Ask for sound only when relevant; review actual audio rather than assuming a pour or crunch is audible.

## Avoid
Do not fabricate health, nutrition, supplement, ingredient, or preparation claims. Do not promise impossible liquid physics or exact portion consistency. Check packaging and labels, utensils, fingers, steam, and the final food shape against the approved references.
