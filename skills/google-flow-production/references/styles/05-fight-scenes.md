# Fight Scenes

## Hook
Establish readable tension: a stance change, a blocked path, or the moment before a controlled exchange. Orient the viewer before accelerating the action.

## Camera
Keep both performers' positions understandable. Use a stable wide or a short lateral track for the main movement; save close-ups for reactions. Preserve action direction across cuts.

## Lighting
Separate silhouettes from the background and keep the active space visible. Dust or motion blur can support impact but should not conceal broken anatomy or impossible positions.

## Beat
Describe a small fictional choreography unit: approach, evade or block, and reaction. Specify starting and ending positions. Leave room for anticipation and recovery instead of demanding many simultaneous strikes.

## Avoid
Do not confuse energetic camera movement with coherent choreography. Review limbs, contact, object continuity, and spatial relationships. Keep the scene within the user's permitted content and the service's rules; do not promise unsafe or disallowed content can be generated.
