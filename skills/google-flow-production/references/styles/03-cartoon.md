# Cartoon

## Hook
Use a clear expression, silhouette, visual contradiction, or a single exaggerated reaction. Let the viewer understand the character's intention immediately.

## Camera
Prefer a stable stage or simple pan when expressive action is important. Use a close-up for a reaction and a wider view when the gag depends on body position.

## Lighting
Choose a coherent visual vocabulary: flat color, textured illustration, paper cutout, or soft shaded forms. Keep line weight, palette, and background detail consistent with the approved references.

## Beat
Set up an expectation, perform one readable action, and hold the reaction. Exaggeration should preserve identity and the product's important features. Use anticipation and follow-through rather than unrelated transformations.

## Avoid
Do not ask for every animation style at once or assume frame-perfect character rigs. References improve direction but do not guarantee pose or identity. Exact lettering and consistent graphic overlays belong in a reviewed edit when required.
